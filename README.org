#+TITLE: itlogs.food
#+STARTUP: showall

Hands-free food logging with Ray-Ban Meta glasses and any kitchen scale. Put the food on the scale, say "log it" (or press the button), and the glasses read the food and the grams off the display, look again a second later, and speak the entry back. When the two looks disagree, it asks and you answer by voice. The entry, with its nutrients, goes to Apple Health.

Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon entry, Indie track, Build for Your World. Everything about the competition is in [[file:hackathon/README.org][hackathon/]].

* Why
- Photo apps guess the portion and underestimate a meal by about a third (NIH metabolic-kitchen test, NUTRITION 2026: 252 to 345 kcal low per meal). Smart scales weigh, then make you pick the food by hand on the phone.
- Meta's glasses can log food from a photo since 2026 (US only, estimates, Meta AI app). They cannot weigh. Most kitchens already have a scale.
- So: the glasses read the number on the scale. Any scale, no pairing, no app on the scale's side, hands free while cooking. See [[file:hackathon/meta-food-logging.org][what Meta has done and said]], with the prior art.

* How it works
1. The phone sends the glasses' camera (one still every 2.5 s, or the video stream) to a server on the LAN. The stream is never written; only the frames a read was made on are kept, and only when recording is on.
2. "Log it" (voice) or the button: the latest frame is read by Muse Spark (Meta Model API) as structured output: =food=, =display_value=, =unit=, =confidence=. Foods you log often are checked first.
3. About 1.5 s later the next frame is read again. Two reads that agree (same food, grams within 1 g) log the entry as *agreed* and the ear hears "87 grams of cooked white rice, logged." Two reads also prove the scale had settled.
4. Reads that disagree become a question in the ear: "cooked white rice: 81 or 87 grams?" The answer ("87", "yes", "quinoa", "no") is heard by Muse Voice Transcribe and parsed by Muse Spark into confirm, correct or cancel. The entry is logged as *confirmed* or *corrected*.
5. Grams × a per-100 g row give kcal, protein, carbs, fat, fiber and sodium. The rows come from the Canadian Nutrient File (Health Canada, 5,690 foods, open licence): a curated map for what people weigh most, a careful name search for the rest, and "?" when a food is not there. Salt is weighed like everything else: 1 g of salt is about 390 mg of sodium, and no camera sees salt.
6. Every entry carries the label the loop produced (agreed, confirmed, corrected). Those labels are the eval set and the recalibration data for the open-weight reader, Muse Glimmer on the Mac (issue #4).

* Engineering
Three machines, one WebSocket, one seam. The glasses see and hear, the phone carries, the Mac on the LAN thinks, and the models sit behind two Python protocols so any of them can be swapped without touching the loop.

- The phone never calls a model and never holds a key: =MODEL_API_KEY= lives on the Mac, and the phone only ever receives text to speak and entries to show.
- Every model call goes through =VisionProvider.read_entry= and =LLMProvider.parse_reply= (=server/itlogs/providers/base.py=). The tests, =make eval= and CI run on =fake.py=; =make run-live= puts the Meta models behind the same two calls. Another model is one new file under =providers/=.
- What is kept: only the frames a read was made on, plus the entries and the questions (=record.py=, on the T7 disk, never in git). The stream itself is never written. Every entry carries the label the loop produced (agreed, confirmed, corrected, spoken, auto), so the eval set and the recalibration data (issue #4) come out of ordinary use.

Weighing mode is the same loop driven by the server. From Start (the button, or "I'm weighing my food") it reads a frame every 1.5 s on its own; two reads in a row that agree on a new value log the food quietly (status =auto=, the phone ticks). Stop, or "done", reads the whole list back with the totals. With "ask me after each food" on, the ear asks "113 grams of apple, right?" instead, and nothing is logged without the wearer's word.
