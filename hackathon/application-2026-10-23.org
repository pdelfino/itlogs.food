* Project idea (option A)
#+begin_src text
Build for Your World. I run Le Day Club, a sports-events company in Montreal (6 events, 171 members). After each event members want their own photos, not a folder of 300. I already sort them by face, but beach and sports photos defeat face recognition: sunglasses, caps, backs of heads, motion blur. In 10 days I will add Meta's models: SAM 3.1 segments every person (body, jersey, gear, not just the face), so people are matched even when the face is hidden; Muse Spark checks uncertain matches and writes each person a short recap of their day; Muse Image makes a personal event card. Output: every attendee gets one email with their photos and recap. Success = fewer unassigned photos than face-only matching, on real event photos I own.
#+end_src

* Technical skill: an AI project I built
#+begin_src text
LDC photo sorter (Python, runs locally). Pipeline: ingest photos plus the attendee list; detect and embed faces with InsightFace (SCRFD detector, ArcFace 512-d embeddings) on onnxruntime; agglomerative clustering on cosine distance, so I label about 30 clusters instead of 200 faces; a Flask labeling app with roster autocomplete, merge and reject actions; a persistent member bank, so returning members come pre-filled at the next event; per-person folders via hardlinks; delivery by email through our Cloudflare worker with unsubscribe handling. Also: Claude API in production for image processing, and 4 years at Tallyfor building pipelines that reconcile accounting data to the cent (#2 committer in a team of 10 coders). Earlier, NLP research at FGV: the first algorithm to pass the Brazilian Bar Exam (2 publications).
#+end_src

** Idea as submitted (field 7)
#+begin_src text
A gym log that fills itself, using Meta Ray-Ban glasses. My Garmin and RingConn record heart rate but not what I actually did: which exercises, how many sets and reps, how long I rested. With the glasses on, I just train. A phone app reads the glasses camera through the Wearables Device Access Toolkit. Muse Spark identifies the exercise from what I see (the dumbbells in my hands, the bench, the mat), the app counts reps from the camera's motion, and the end of each set starts a rest timer that speaks in my ear ("90 seconds, set 3 of 4 next"). I can correct anything by voice ("that was 8"). At the end, the whole session (exercises, sets, reps, rest) is written into the Strava activity my Garmin already recorded. Theme: Build for Your World. I run a four-day split at home and lose count of my reps every session.
#+end_src

** Technical skill (field 8, the 786-character version; the box garbled anything longer)
#+begin_src text
A face-sorting pipeline for event photos (Python, 2026) for Le Day Club (https://www.leday.club), a sports-events side project I run in Montreal. It is AI because the core is deep-learning computer vision: neural networks detect every face and turn it into an embedding, so the same person is recognized across photos despite sunglasses, caps and motion blur. Each member gets a folder with only their photos. Two events so far: 1,253 photos, 2,537 faces, 37 members in the identity bank. Techniques: InsightFace (SCRFD + ArcFace embeddings) on onnxruntime; unsupervised clustering on cosine distance (1,217 faces became 105 groups to label); a Flask human-in-the-loop UI; returning members pre-matched next event. Fully local. Day job: #2 committer in a team of 10 coders at Tallyfor.
